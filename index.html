        5 Good to know

        Where you charge decides everything

        At home an EV costs a fraction of petrol per km. On fast chargers only, the gap narrows a lot. If you can't charge at home or work, run the "No home charging" mix above before you buy.

        Solar and time-of-use

        Charging in the day from your own panels, or overnight on a time-of-use tariff such as Eskom's Homeflex, can cut the home price a lot. Try R0 or your off-peak rate in the home box.

        Speed costs EVs more

        Air drag rises with the square of speed. At 120 km/h an EV can use a lot more than its spec-sheet figure; slowing to 100 km/h can save a charging stop on a long trip.

        Outages

        A home charger needs power when you plug in. If load shedding, load reduction or outages hit your area, plan charging around the schedule or charge from a battery and solar system.

        Check the battery warranty

        For a used EV especially: how many years and km it has left, and what capacity it guarantees. Ask for a battery health report before you buy.

        Public charging needs apps

        Most networks need their own app or card and an account with money in it. Set them up before a trip, and check for idle fees if you stay plugged in after charging.

        Common questions

        Is an electric car cheaper to run than petrol in South Africa?

        Charged at home, usually by a long way: an EV costs a fraction of petrol per km. On public fast chargers only, the gap narrows a lot. The calculator above compares the two at today's official fuel price and your own electricity price; if you can't charge at home or work, try the "No home charging" mix.

        How much does it cost to charge an EV in South Africa?

        It depends on where you charge: at home on your electricity tariff, or at a public charger, where fast DC charging costs the most. The prices this page uses are under "Where the numbers come from".

        Does driving fast use more battery?

        Yes. Air drag rises with the square of speed, so at 120 km/h an EV can use a lot more than its spec-sheet figure. Slowing to 100 km/h can save a charging stop on a long trip.

        Can I charge an EV from solar, or during load shedding?

        Charging in the day from your own panels, or overnight on a time-of-use tariff such as Eskom's Homeflex, can cut the home price a lot. A home charger needs power when you plug in, so during outages, plan charging around the schedule or charge from a battery and solar system.

        What should I check before buying a used EV?

        The battery warranty: how many years and km it has left, and what capacity it guarantees. Ask for a battery health report before you buy. Then run the car through sections 3 and 4 above to see whether it pays.
